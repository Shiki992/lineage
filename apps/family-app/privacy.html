Lineage

Privacy

Last changed 21 October 2026.

Lineage is free, open-source software for keeping track of your Word documents, PowerPoint decks and Excel workbooks. It has no servers of its own, no Lineage accounts, no analytics and no advertising. This page says what Lineage reads, where it keeps things, and who else is involved.

What Lineage keeps, and where

  • Your history: checkpoints of your files, your tailored versions, the copies you send and who you sent them to. All of it is in your own OneDrive, in the folder Apps › Lineage. The Lineage project can't see it.
  • Your name, from your Microsoft account, so the checkpoints you save say who saved them. It is kept only in your history.
  • Sign-in: Microsoft's sign-in library keeps your sign-in in the browser storage of Word, PowerPoint, Excel or this site, on your device. Signing out of My documents clears it there.

What Lineage reads

  • The document open in Word, PowerPoint or Excel, while Lineage's panel is open.
  • The files in Apps › Lineage.
  • In Excel on the web: the saved copy of the workbook you have open, from OneDrive.
  • A file you choose to bring in, such as a copy someone sent back to you. Lineage never runs macros or queries in any file; it reads their code as text.

The permissions Lineage asks Microsoft for

Permission When it's asked for What Lineage does with it
Files.ReadWrite.AppFolder When you first sign in Reads and writes Apps › Lineage, and nothing else in your OneDrive.
Files.Read The first time you use Lineage in Excel on the web Reads the workbook you have open, as last saved. Microsoft's screen says this covers all your files; Lineage reads only the workbook that's open.
Files.ReadWrite The first time you share a copy as a link, and only if Microsoft won't let Lineage share from its own folder without it Makes and removes sharing links for the copies you choose. Microsoft's screen says this covers all your files; Lineage shares only copies in Apps › Lineage.

Microsoft also gives Lineage your name and account name when you sign in (the openid and profile permissions). Without the last two permissions, everything else in Lineage still works.

Copies you send or share

  • A copy Lineage makes for you to send is clean: no history, comments, tracked changes or hidden data. It carries only a Lineage ID (two random numbers) so Lineage can recognise it if it comes back. You can turn even that off.
  • When you share a copy as a link, whoever has the link can open it, or people in your organisation if that's how your organisation's OneDrive is set up. An edit link lets them change that copy. Stop sharing removes the link.

Who else is involved

  • Microsoft runs sign-in, OneDrive and Office. Their part is covered by the Microsoft Privacy Statement.
  • Azure Static Web Apps, a Microsoft service, serves Lineage's program files (these pages and Lineage's code). Like any web host, it handles the address your browser connects from in order to send them. The Lineage project doesn't add analytics or request logging to it.
  • Nobody else. Lineage doesn't sell, share or rent anything about you.

Removing Lineage and everything it kept

  1. In OneDrive, open Apps › Lineage. Your tailored versions and sent copies are in there, so move out anything you want to keep, then delete the folder.
  2. Take away Lineage's access: for a personal account at account.live.com/consent/Manage, for a work or school account at myapplications.microsoft.com.
  3. Remove the add-in: in Word, PowerPoint or Excel, go to Home › Add-ins, then My Add-ins, and remove Lineage.

Changes and questions

When this page changes, the date at the top changes too, and every earlier version is in the project's history. Ask a question on GitHub, or report a security problem privately as the security policy describes.
